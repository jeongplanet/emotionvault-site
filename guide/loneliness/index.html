How to Cope with Loneliness: What to Do When You Feel Alone

Things you can do when loneliness feels heavy: name it, make small connections, and turn time alone into time for yourself.

Loneliness happens to everyone

You can feel lonely in a crowded room or with people you love. It doesn't mean something is wrong with you. It's a signal that you need connection, the same way hunger is a signal that you need food.

Name the kind of lonely

Do you miss a specific person, wish someone understood you, or just want company? Naming it points you to what would actually help. Missing someone might mean reaching out, while wanting to be understood might mean writing it down first.

Make small connections

A short message to a friend you haven't talked to in a while, a few words with the barista you see every morning, a comment to a neighbor. These moments seem small, but they add up. You don't have to start with a deep conversation.

Fill time alone with things you enjoy

Take a walk, put on music you like, cook something or do something with your hands. Time alone can turn into time for yourself rather than time you just get through.

Write a letter to yourself

Write to yourself on a lonely day, or to the person you'll be a year from now. It can feel like keeping yourself company. If loneliness lasts a long time or starts to feel hopeless, talking to a counselor or doctor can help.
